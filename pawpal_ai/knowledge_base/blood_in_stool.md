## Two kinds of visible blood (paraphrased)
Hematochezia is bright red blood, usually from the lower tract (colon, rectum, anus). Mild cases may show small streaks of bright red blood on otherwise normal stool.
Melena is black, tar-like stool, where blood has been swallowed or digested, which points toward the upper digestive tract (stomach, small intestine). Diet can also darken stool (for example organ meats or dark proteins), so melena can be hard to judge by eye. The article suggests wiping stool on a paper towel to see whether it looks dark red or black.

## When to contact a veterinarian
The article advises contacting a veterinarian whenever blood appears in stool, and immediately if the dog is lethargic, vomiting, refusing food or water, has pale gums, or has large volumes of bloody diarrhea. It lists large amounts of blood, dark tarry stool, jelly-like bloody diarrhea, frequent straining, or blood without stool as serious signs. If a dog is unvaccinated or may have been exposed to parvovirus, call ahead before visiting a clinic.
